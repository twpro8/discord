## Why Lumiere?

Most chat platforms ask you to trust someone else with your community. Your servers, your history, your members, your uptime — all sitting on infrastructure you don't control and can't inspect, subject to rules you didn't write.

Lumiere takes a different approach: **run it yourself.**

It's built for people who would rather host their own infrastructure than rent access to someone else's — friend groups, gaming communities, teams, open-source projects, or organizations that want a communication platform that answers to them, not to a Terms of Service page. In that sense, Lumiere sits closer to projects like [Jellyfin](https://jellyfin.org/), [Forgejo](https://forgejo.org/), [Matrix](https://matrix.org/), and [Nextcloud](https://nextcloud.com/) than it does to any commercial chat SaaS: software you deploy, run, modify, and trust because you can see how it works.

That philosophy shapes every decision in this project:

- **Ownership** — if you run Lumiere, the deployment, the data, and the decisions about it are yours.
- **Independence** — no proprietary backend, no vendor lock-in, no dependency on a company staying in business.
- **Simplicity** — self-hosting should be realistic for a small team or a single hobbyist, not just a large org with dedicated ops staff.
- **Transparency** — the whole stack is open source; there is nothing about how Lumiere works that isn't inspectable.
- **Extensibility** — a modular architecture that's meant to be built on, forked, and extended.
- **Community** — Lumiere is developed in the open, and it improves through contributions, not a roadmap dictated top-down.

We're intentionally careful not to oversell this: self-hosting Lumiere means *you* are responsible for how you deploy and secure it, and we won't make blanket privacy or security guarantees a self-hosted project can't back up. What we can promise is openness — the code is yours to read, run, and change.

## About

Lumiere is a communication platform bringing messaging, communities, and (eventually) real-time voice and video into one self-hostable app. It follows a model people already know from modern chat platforms — servers, channels, direct messages — but as software you deploy on your own infrastructure instead of an account you rent from a provider.

The project is currently in **MVP stage**. Messaging, servers, channels, and authentication are implemented and functional; voice, video, and screen sharing are on the roadmap and not yet available. This README reflects that distinction accurately throughout.
